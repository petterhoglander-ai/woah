VO₂max on Apple Watch: what the number means, why it's probably low, and how to raise it

By Petter Höglander5 November 202610 min read

VO₂max on Apple Watch, called Cardio Fitness in the Health app, is an estimate of how much oxygen your body can use when you go all out. The watch guesses it from how fast your heart beats at a given pace on outdoor walks, runs and hikes. Read it as a trend, not as a lab result, because for most people it reads a few points low. And it is worth caring about: it is the longevity number you can move the most, often 5 to 10% in a couple of months.

Below: where it comes from, what a normal number is for your age, how accurate it is, why it suddenly drops, and what actually raises it.

How does Apple Watch measure VO₂max?

It never measures it. A real VO₂max test is done on a treadmill with a mask over your face, running until you can't anymore. Your watch does something much easier: it looks at your heart rate and your pace during a walk or run that isn't all-out, and predicts what your maximum would be.

According to Apple's own white paper, a session counts when:

  • it's an Outdoor Walk, Outdoor Run or Hiking workout (or an outdoor walk the watch picks up on its own),
  • the ground is fairly flat, less than a 5% slope,
  • GPS and the heart signal are good,
  • and your heart rate goes up about 30% of the way from your resting rate to your max.

Your first such workout gives you nothing, and the watch needs to have been worn for a day. Indoor workouts, cycling and the gym never count. The number lives in the Health app under Heart, Cardio Fitness, and it runs from 14 to 65 ml/kg/min.

That last rule matters more than people think. If all you do outdoors is a slow walk, the watch may not have enough to go on.

What is a good VO₂max for my age?

It depends on your age and sex, and honestly, on which country you compare against. Apple grades you against the US FRIEND registry, which is built from real treadmill tests. These are the middle values from it (Kaminsky et al., 2015):

Apple's "Low" level is the bottom fifth for your age and sex if you are 20 to 59. From 60 it is a fixed line instead: 18 for men and 15 for women, roughly what you need to keep living independently.

Here's the part nobody mentions. In the same paper, the average man in his 20s tested at about 48 in the US. In a large Norwegian study, it was about 54. So "average" is not a fact about your body. It's a fact about who you are compared with.

That's why I don't care much about the label. I care about one comparison: you, against you three months ago.

How accurate is Apple Watch VO₂max?

Good at the trend, not so good at the level. And the two studies that matter say different things for a reason.

Apple's own validation (221 people) found the watch was on average 1.4 ml/kg/min off, and very consistent from one estimate to the next. But Apple compared the watch with another submaximal prediction, not with a real all-out test.

The independent one did the real test. In Lambe and colleagues (PLOS ONE, 2025), 30 people wore an Apple Watch for 5 to 10 days and then ran to exhaustion on a treadmill with a mask. The watch read 6 ml/kg/min lower on average, with an average error of about 13%. For some people it was much further off.

You will read online that Apple Watch overestimates VO₂max. The only independent lab study I could find says the opposite for most people. It's a small study, so I wouldn't treat 6 as a fixed correction. But if your watch says "Below Average" and you feel fit, there's a fair chance you are fitter than it thinks.

What I would trust: the direction over months, as long as you keep doing the same kind of outdoor sessions.

Why did my VO₂max suddenly drop?

This is the question I see most, especially since watchOS 26. Before you assume you got unfit, go through this list. Most sudden drops are the measurement, not you.

  1. Apple recalculated your history. With iOS 26, the Health app can offer to update earlier Cardio Fitness estimates when it thinks they could be more accurate. You get a prompt, and the change can't be undone. People on Apple's forums report moves of a few points both ways after updates. Apple doesn't say what changed.
  2. Your Health details are wrong or old. Age, sex, weight and height all go into the estimate. A weight from three years ago is enough to shift it.
  3. You take a medication that limits heart rate and haven't told Health. For beta blockers and calcium channel blockers this is big: in Apple's data the error dropped from about 12 to under 2 ml/kg/min once the medication was entered. You add it under Health Details.
  4. Your heart was working harder for reasons the watch can't see. Heat, dehydration, a lot of coffee, a trip to altitude. All of them push heart rate up at the same pace, and the watch reads that as lower fitness.
  5. The route made you work harder. Hills, sand, snow, a heavy backpack, pushing a stroller. The watch doesn't know.
  6. Your sessions got easier. If your outdoor walks are slow and your hard training moved indoors, the estimate has less to work with.

And sometimes it is you. Fitness drops faster than it builds. Apple's own paper notes that inactivity can take VO₂max down by up to 27% in two to three weeks. So a fortnight off with a cold, followed by a lower number, is probably real. The good news is it usually comes back about as fast.

How do I get more readings, and better ones?

  • Start an Outdoor Walk or Outdoor Run workout instead of just going for a walk.
  • Pick a flat route and use the same one most of the time, so the number compares like with like.
  • Get your heart rate up properly at least once a week. A brisk walk where talking takes some effort is enough for most people.
  • Wear the watch through the day, not just for workouts.
  • Keep Health Details up to date.

If you use Garmin: on my own Garmin, its VO₂max never reached Apple Health. Garmin shows it in Garmin Connect, but apps that read Apple Health won't see it.

How do you actually raise VO₂max?

Hard intervals, mostly. This is one of the clearer findings in exercise science.

In a classic study by Helgerud and colleagues (2007), 40 moderately trained men trained three times a week for eight weeks. Every group did the same total amount of work, just at different intensities. The group doing 4 × 4 minutes at 90 to 95% of max heart rate, with 3 minutes easy in between, improved VO₂max by 7.2%, from 55.5 to 60.4. The groups doing long easy runs or steady threshold runs didn't improve significantly.

Apple's paper sums up the wider research the same way: programmes of 6 to 12 weeks usually give 5 to 10%, and high-intensity intervals give the most.

What I would do, if you don't train much today:

  • One interval session a week. 4 × 4 minutes where talking is hard, 3 minutes easy between. Uphill walking works if running doesn't.
  • Everything else easy. Walks, easy runs, cycling. They don't move the watch number much, but they build the base that makes the hard session possible.
  • Give it eight weeks before you judge it. One run tells you nothing.

One honest thing: genes explain a lot of how much people respond. Apple's paper cites 20 to 60% of the difference in improvement. If you move less than your friend on the same plan, that's normal, and it's not a reason to stop. The benefit for your health doesn't depend on winning.

Why bother?

Because it's the strongest longevity number you can change. I ranked it first in which health metrics actually matter for longevity, and the reason is not only that fit people live longer. It's that getting fitter seems to count too.

In a Finnish study (Laukkanen et al., 2016), 579 men had a real VO₂max test twice, 11 years apart. For every 1 ml/kg/min they held on to or gained, their risk of death in the following years was 9% lower. That's observational, so it doesn't prove cause. But it points the same way as everything else.

And it matters more every decade. In the Baltimore Longitudinal Study of Aging (Fleg et al., 2005), VO₂max fell about 3 to 6% per decade in people's 20s and 30s, and more than 20% per decade from their 70s. The fitness you build at 45 is what you have to spend at 75.

How I show it in Woah

When I built Woah's VO₂max card I had to make a few choices, and I think they apply to reading the number anywhere.

Woah shows your VO₂max next to where it places you for your age and sex, in five bands: Building, Fair, Average, Strong and Excellent. Five, because the measurement's own error is a few points wide, and a finer scale would pretend to precision the watch doesn't have. For the same reason the percentile is rounded to 5 and never goes beyond "top 5%". Nobody's watch knows they are in the top 2%.

The bottom band is called Building, not Poor. The person at the bottom is the person with the most to gain, and I don't think a label should make them feel like they failed a test.

It grades against Cooper Institute and ACSM norms, not FRIEND, so it can place you a little differently than Apple does. Neither is wrong. They compare you with different people.

And VO₂max is never part of Woah's daily recovery score. It moves over months, the recovery score answers a question about today, and mixing the two would make both less honest. It sits in "Heart, answered", it's free, and if your watch hasn't made an estimate yet, the card says so and tells you how to get one. Asking the coach what to do about it is the paid part. Questions people ask

Where do I find VO₂max on Apple Watch?

In the Health app on your iPhone, under Heart, then Cardio Fitness. It needs a day of wear and a few Outdoor Walk, Outdoor Run or Hiking workouts on fairly flat ground before the first estimate appears.

Is Apple Watch VO₂max accurate?

It is consistent, but often low. Apple's own validation found an average error of 1.4 ml/kg/min against another estimate, while an independent 2025 study against a real treadmill test found the watch read about 6 ml/kg/min low on average, with about 13% error. Use it for the trend over months.

Why did my Apple Watch VO₂max drop suddenly?

Most sudden drops are the measurement: a recalculation of earlier estimates after an iOS update, outdated weight or age in Health, a heart-rate-limiting medication that isn't entered, heat, caffeine, hills or easier sessions. A real drop is possible too, since two to three weeks of inactivity can lower VO₂max a lot.

What is a good VO₂max for my age?

In the US FRIEND registry, the median is 48.0 for men and 37.6 for women in their 20s, falling to 32.6 and 23.4 in their 50s. Other countries have higher averages, so the most useful comparison is your own number three months ago.

How fast can you improve VO₂max?

Training programmes of 6 to 12 weeks usually raise VO₂max by 5 to 10%. In one study, four 4-minute intervals at 90 to 95% of max heart rate, three times a week, raised it 7.2% in eight weeks.
