Best recovery apps for Apple Watch (2026): an honest comparison

By Petter Höglander3 September 202610 min read

Apple Watch collects everything a recovery score needs and then does not give you one. So you install an app. Here are the ones worth your time, ranked, with prices I checked today. One of them is mine, so read that part with that in mind.

A quick note on why I wrote this. Search "best recovery app for Apple Watch" and almost every page you get is written by one of the apps. Livity's list ranks Livity first. Ensta's list ranks Ensta first. Mine ranks mine fourth. Make of that what you want, but I have been building sleep and fitness apps for years and I have watched this category grow from one app to a dozen, so I at least know what they all do with your data.

Does Apple Watch have a recovery score?

No. The watch measures HRV, resting heart rate, sleep stages, breathing rate, wrist temperature and every workout, and it never combines them into a number that says how ready you are today. Since watchOS 26 (September 2025) you get a Sleep Score from 0 to 100, and the Vitals app tells you when an overnight signal is outside your usual range. Both are good. Neither answers the question people actually have in the morning: can I train, or should I take it easy?

That gap is the reason every app below exists. They all read the same data from Apple Health. The difference is what they do with it.

If HRV is new to you, read why your HRV goes down first. It explains the number all of these apps are built on.

How I compared them

Four things, in this order.

  1. What it actually reads. Everyone reads Apple Health, so the raw data is the same. But some apps also pull workouts from Garmin or Strava, or sleep from Oura, and that matters if you own more than one device.
  2. Whether it tells you what to do. A score is not an answer. Some apps stop at the number, some go further.
  3. What it costs. Every price below is what the US App Store listing showed on 3 September 2026. Prices change and they differ by country, so treat the numbers as "roughly this" and check your own store before you decide.
  4. Who it is for. Because the honest answer to "which is best" is "for whom".

I did not rank on ratings, but I list them. 11,000 ratings at 4.8 tells you something that 9 ratings at 4.7 does not.

One thing to keep in mind through the whole list: same data, different math. Athlytic's 71 and Bevel's 71 are not the same 71. Every app builds its own baseline, weighs sleep and HRV differently, and calls a different range "ready". Pick one and stay with it for a month. Switching every week means you never get a baseline in any of them.

1. Athlytic: the best recovery app for Apple Watch if you train

Athlytic is what most people mean when they say "Whoop on an Apple Watch", and it has earned that. It gives you a Recovery score from 0 to 100 built on HRV and resting heart rate against your own baseline, an Exertion score from 0 to 10 that builds through the day as you move, and a target exertion zone for the day that matches your recovery. Sleep gets its own read with quality, debt and consistency. It also watches your overnight wrist temperature, blood oxygen and breathing rate and tells you when one of them is off.

It reads Apple Health, imports workouts from Garmin Connect, Strava, Wahoo and Peloton, and takes sleep from Oura if you wear a ring at night. Everything is processed on your phone and the App Store privacy label says "Data Not Collected". It is made by two people.

Price: $29.99 a year or $4.99 a month, with a 7-day trial. That is the cheapest serious option on this list. 4.8 stars from 11,000 ratings.

What it is not: quiet. Athlytic is a dashboard. A good one, but the morning is a screen of numbers and it is up to you to turn them into a decision. If you like that, and a lot of people who train do, this is your app.

2. Bevel: the most you can get without paying

Bevel gives away more than anyone else here. Recovery, sleep, strain and stress scores, an energy bank, a biological age, a journal, a strength builder with 700 exercises and nutrition logging against a database of 6 million foods. All free. What you pay for is Bevel Intelligence, the AI coach that talks to you about it: $14.99 a month or $99.99 a year, or credit packs from $4.99 to $49.99 if you would rather pay per use.

It reads Apple Health and connects to Garmin Connect, Oura and Google Health (which is how Fitbit gets in). 4.8 stars from 16,000 ratings, the most on this list.

What it is not: focused. Bevel is everything, which means more screens than any other app here. If you open an app to check one number and leave, Bevel will wear on you. If you want one app instead of four and you are fine with paying nothing and ignoring half of it, it is the best deal in the category.

3. Gentler Streak: best if you do not want to train by numbers

Gentler Streak is not really a recovery score app, and that is why it is here. Instead of a number it draws a path: a band of effort your body can handle right now, based on your recent training and how you slept. Stay inside it and you are fine. Push above it and there is a countdown to overreaching. It also has a readiness read, a 14-night sleep trend, a vitals summary that flags changes before you feel them, and cycle tracking for people who have one. You can mark yourself as ill or resting without breaking a streak. That last detail is the whole philosophy.

It won Apple Watch App of the Year in 2022 and an Apple Design Award in 2024, and it is built by a small team that has kept its opinion for five years.

Price: $8.99 a month, $39.99 a year, or lifetime from $59.99 (the listing shows several lifetime tiers, so what you see may differ). 4.7 stars from 8,800 ratings.

What it is not: for the numbers person. If you want to see your HRV every morning and argue with it, Gentler Streak will frustrate you, because it deliberately hides the arithmetic.

4. Woah: fewer numbers, one answer, and someone to share it with

I built Woah, so read this part with a raised eyebrow.

The idea is simple. Apple Health already has your sleep, your HRV, your resting heart rate, your breathing rate and your activity. Woah reads all of that and gives you one recovery score in the morning, the reasoning behind it, and a block called "Today, answered" with the questions you actually have: can I train today, how well did I sleep, how far behind on sleep am I, should I drink tonight, am I getting sick, should I sleep in tomorrow. The answers come from your numbers, not from an AI making things up. Every night also gets a sleep score in stars and a picture of the night in the order it happened, and there is an illness radar that reads your overnight signals against your own baselines on a 1 to 10 scale.

The model is what I spent the most time on. Your baseline is your own last 30 days. A falling 3-day HRV trend does not just lower today's score, it lowers the ceiling of what the score can be. And the drinks you logged last night cap the score this morning, because a body that had three beers is not fully ready no matter how the watch read the sleep. I wrote about the reasoning in the HRV article.

Then the part no other app on this list has: you can pair with a partner. A 6-character code, and each of you sees how the other slept and how ready their day is, without asking. You choose what is shared. It sounds small until you have it.

The free core is the daily score, the answers, the sleep stars and stages, habits and streaks, the last two weeks of every chart, the longevity estimate, and pairing. Premium is the long view: fitness age, VO2max, your sleep regularity and social jetlag over the month, the full history, the coach with a weekly plan, and the depth on the partner side. The price is a subscription and it is shown in the app before anything is charged. I do not print it in articles because it differs by country and I would rather you saw the real one.

What it is not good at, honestly: there is no strain or training-load score. If you want to watch your exertion build during a ride, that is Athlytic. It is iPhone only, no Android. It is younger and has far fewer ratings than everything above it. And it works with Garmin, Oura and Whoop only through Apple Health, with no direct sync.

Why fourth: if you train seriously, Athlytic does more. If you want everything for free, Bevel does more. Gentler Streak has been doing its one thing for five years with the awards to show for it. Fourth, and not lower, because nothing above it will tell you what to do with the number, or let the person you live with see it. How the score is built is in the FAQ.

5. Livity: does a lot, and I cannot tell you what it costs

Livity is the broadest app here after Bevel. Recovery and readiness from HRV, resting heart rate, sleep and recent training, a body battery, sleep stages with a sleep score and a sleep coach, training load, stress, fitness age, and an AI coach. Calculations happen on your phone. The thing it does that nobody above it does: it syncs directly with Garmin, Fitbit and Oura, so you do not need an Apple Watch at all. For Garmin owners, whose HRV history in Apple Health is patchy at best, that is a real reason to pick it.

4.7 stars from 1,100 ratings. The free core covers recovery, body battery and sleep.

Now the price. The App Store listing shows Pro at $9.99 a month, $59.99 a year, a second yearly tier at $79.99, a weekly at $3.99 and a lifetime at $149.99. Livity's own blog says Premium is $39.99 a year. Both may be true depending on which offer you get, but I could not tell you which one you will see. Open the app and check before you decide. That is the whole reason it is fifth: it does most of what Athlytic and Bevel do, with less clarity about what it is and what it costs.

6. Training Today: one number, on your wrist, and nothing else

Training Today is the oldest app on this list and the simplest. It gives you a readiness-to-train score from HRV, resting heart rate and your workouts, and since version 5.1 in July 2026 it also looks at your recent sleep against your normal and any sleep debt. The score lives on the watch as a complication and the watch app works without the phone. That is the entire product.

Price: $2.95 a month, $29.99 a year or $59.99 once, for life. 4.6 stars from 2,600 ratings.

What it is not: an explanation. There is no sleep analysis, no reasoning, no coaching. You get the number and you decide. For some people that is exactly right, and if you are one of them the lifetime price makes it the cheapest app here by a mile.

Two more worth knowing about

Cora is a training and nutrition coach with a recovery score (they call it Body Charge) inside. AI-generated workout plans that adapt to recovery, macro tracking, a live workout tracker. 4.8 stars from 438 ratings. The listing shows two in-app purchases at $14.99 and $79.99 but does not label the billing period, so check the App Store. If what you want is a plan rather than a score, look here. If what you want is recovery, it is a lot of app for that.

Ensta turns your data into an Energy score from 0 to 100 with recovery, strain, stress and a bio age behind it, and it runs on Android too through Health Connect. $10.99 a month or $69.99 a year. 4.7 stars, from 9 ratings, which is why it is not ranked: too new to judge. Worth watching if you or someone in your life is on Android.

Which recovery app should you pick?

The short version.

  • You train hard and want strain and recovery together: Athlytic.
  • You want the most for free and do not mind the clutter: Bevel.
  • You are tired of scores and want to keep moving without guilt: Gentler Streak.
  • You want one answer in the morning, and maybe your partner's: Woah.
  • You wear a Garmin and no Apple Watch: Livity.
  • You want a number on the watch face and nothing else: Training Today.

Whatever you pick, give it 30 days. Every app on this list needs a baseline before its score means anything about you, and the baseline takes weeks. Switching every week is the one way to make all six of them useless.

Questions people ask

Does Apple Watch have a built-in recovery score?

No. As of watchOS 26 you get a Sleep Score from 0 to 100 and the Vitals app, which flags overnight signals outside your usual range. Nothing on the watch combines HRV, resting heart rate, sleep and training into a readiness or recovery number. That is what the apps in this comparison add.

Which recovery app for Apple Watch is free?

Bevel gives away the most: recovery, sleep, strain, stress and biological age at no cost, with the AI coach as the paid part. Athlytic, Gentler Streak, Livity, Training Today and Woah all have a free core with a paid tier on top. Training Today is the only one with a one-time lifetime price, $59.99 on 3 September 2026.

Are Apple Watch recovery apps accurate?

As accurate as the watch. Apple Watch samples HRV for short periods rather than all night and reports SDNN, so a single reading is noisy and the trend is what matters. Every app here computes from the same Apple Health data with different math, which is why the same night can be a 65 in one app and a 78 in another. Compare yourself with your own baseline in one app, not across apps.

Can I use these apps without an Apple Watch?

Mostly yes. All of them read Apple Health, so any device that writes sleep and heart data there works: Garmin, Oura, Whoop, Polar, Fitbit. Livity and Bevel also sync directly with Garmin and Oura, and Ensta runs on Android through Health Connect.
