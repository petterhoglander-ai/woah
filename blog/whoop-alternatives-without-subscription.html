Best WHOOP alternatives without a subscription (2026)

By Petter Höglander24 September 20268 min read

The cheapest WHOOP alternative is probably already on your wrist. If you have an Apple Watch, a free app gives you a recovery score from the data it already collects. If you have a Series 12 or an Ultra 4, Apple now gives you one built in. And if you do not have a watch at all, there are now three screenless bands you buy once and never pay for again.

WHOOP is a good product. But it is a membership, not a purchase. Here is what the alternatives actually cost, what you give up, and which one I would pick in your situation. All prices are US prices, checked on 24 September 2026.

What WHOOP costs, for reference

WHOOP has three memberships, and the band comes with them:

  • WHOOP One: $199 a year, or $25 a month.
  • WHOOP Peak: $239 a year, or $30 a month.
  • WHOOP Life: $359 a year, or $40 a month.

Over three years that is $597 to $1,077, and you never own anything outright. That is the number every alternative below is really competing with.

"Without a subscription" means three different things

Most lists mix these up, and it is the actual decision:

  1. A band you buy once. Hardware with no monthly fee. Good if you do not have a watch, or do not want to sleep in one.
  2. A free app on the watch you already have. No new hardware at all. The app reads Apple Health and does the recovery maths.
  3. An app you pay for once. Same as 2, but with a lifetime price instead of a subscription.

Group 1: bands you buy once

These are the closest thing to a WHOOP: no screen, worn 24/7, sleep and heart data, recovery-style scores.

  • Amazfit Helio Strap, $99.99. The cheapest by far. No required subscription, and it can sync to Apple Health. If you just want to try this whole recovery thing without spending much, this is where I would start.
  • Polar Loop, $199.90. Polar has done heart rate for decades, and it shows. No subscription, up to 8 days of battery, and it can share data to Apple Health through Polar Flow.
  • Garmin Cirqa, $199.99. New since July. Sleep score, Body Battery, stress, skin temperature, up to 10 days of battery, no subscription. You get Garmin's whole app with it, which is a lot of data. Maybe too much, depending on who you are.

The honest downside of all three: they are one more device to charge and one more app to open. If you already wear an Apple Watch, you probably do not need a second thing on your wrist.

Group 2: the watch you already have, plus an app

If you own an Apple Watch, it is already measuring the things a recovery score is made of: sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, breathing rate, workouts. The data is sitting in Apple Health. What is missing is someone comparing last night with your own normal. That is all a recovery app really does.

Apple Watch Readiness. If you have a Series 12 or an Ultra 4, start here. It is built in, it costs nothing, and it has been in stores since 18 September. It needs 7 nights of sleep tracking before the first score. It only runs on those two models. I wrote a whole piece on what a low Readiness score means.

Bevel. Gives away the most. Recovery, sleep and strain are free, and the paid part is mainly the AI side. If you want the widest free app and do not mind a lot of screens, Bevel is a strong pick.

Livity. Recovery, sleep, HRV trends and a body battery on a free tier, with deeper analytics and history behind the subscription. Also has a one-time price, $149.99, if you would rather pay once.

Woah. I built this one, so read this part with a raised eyebrow. Woah reads Apple Health, so it works with any Apple Watch, and with Garmin, Oura, Polar and others that write to Apple Health. Every morning you get one recovery score, locked for the day, what moved it, and every chart behind it: sleep, HRV and resting heart rate against your own normal, trends over weeks and months, fitness age and VO₂max.

All of that is free, with no time limit. Everything Woah calculates on your phone costs nothing. What you pay for is the written part: the plain-words read on why today looks the way it does, and the coach. The price is shown in the app before anything is charged. What it is not good at: there is no strain score, and it is iPhone only. Woah on the App Store.

Group 3: pay once for an app

Training Today, $59.99 lifetime. The only one here where you can pay once and be done for a fair price. It gives you a daily "readiness to train" from your Apple Watch data, and it is simple on purpose. If the word "subscription" is the whole problem for you, this is the answer.

Athlytic does not really belong on a no-subscription list, it is $29.99 a year. But it is the cheapest subscription in the category and one of the best apps, which is why I ranked it first in my comparison of recovery apps for Apple Watch.

What nobody tells you before you switch

Your baseline does not come with you. Every recovery score compares last night with your own normal, and that normal is learned from the device you wear. A new band or a new app has to learn you again. Apple needs 7 nights for a first score. Woah uses a 30-day baseline. For the first couple of weeks, treat any new score as a rough draft.

The numbers will not match your WHOOP. A 60 on one scale is not a 60 on another, and different devices measure HRV in different ways and at different times of night. Don't compare the new number with the old one. Compare the new number with itself, over a few weeks.

What you actually lose. WHOOP's strain and sleep coaching are good, and the band is comfortable to sleep in. A watch you have to charge every day will miss some nights. If those things matter to you, one of the bands is a better swap than an app.

"Free" usually means "free core". Every free app on this list, mine included, gives you the numbers and sells something on top, usually the AI part. That is fine. Just know which part you are getting for free before you get attached to the other part.

So which one would I pick?

  • You have an Apple Watch Series 12 or Ultra 4: Apple's Readiness. Add an app later only if you want to see what has been moving the number over months.
  • You have any other Apple Watch: a free app. Bevel if you want the most features free. Woah if you want one clear answer in the morning and the why behind it, free. Training Today if you want to pay once and never think about it.
  • You have a Garmin, Oura or Polar: keep using it, and let an app read it through Apple Health if its own app does not give you the answer you want.
  • You have no watch and do not want one: the Amazfit Helio Strap if you want to try it cheaply, the Polar Loop or the Garmin Cirqa if you want to keep it for years.

None of these is a medical device, and neither is WHOOP. They are all ways of noticing, earlier, that you slept badly, trained hard or are coming down with something. For that, the watch you already have is usually enough.

Questions people ask

Is there a WHOOP alternative without a subscription?

Yes. Screenless bands you buy once: Amazfit Helio Strap ($99.99), Polar Loop ($199.90) and Garmin Cirqa ($199.99), none with a required subscription (prices checked 24 September 2026). If you already have an Apple Watch, a free recovery app or Apple's built-in Readiness (Series 12 and Ultra 4) does the same job with no new hardware.

Can an Apple Watch replace a WHOOP?

For recovery, mostly yes. An Apple Watch already measures sleep, HRV, resting heart rate and breathing rate and writes them to Apple Health. Series 12 and Ultra 4 have Apple's own Readiness score, and on older models a recovery app reads the same data and compares it with your own baseline. What you lose is 24/7 wear on nights you charge the watch.

How much does WHOOP cost per year?

In the US, WHOOP One is $199 a year, Peak $239 and Life $359, with the band included (checked 24 September 2026). Monthly billing is $25, $30 and $40. Over three years that is $597 to $1,077.

What is the cheapest alternative to WHOOP?

If you already own an Apple Watch, a free recovery app, which costs nothing. If you need hardware, the Amazfit Helio Strap at $99.99 with no required subscription.

Will my recovery score be the same if I switch from WHOOP?

No. Each device and app uses its own scale and learns your baseline from its own measurements, so give a new one two to four weeks before you trust it, and compare it with itself, not with your old WHOOP number.
